# Review Guidelines

<div style="text-align: justify">
The submitted papers will go through a full, single-blind reviewing process via OpenReview, with two weeks for author rebuttal and discussion. Reviewers will be responsible of approximately 3 to 5 papers.
</div>

## What makes a good MIDL review and what is expected?

- Reviewers should always provide positive, constructive reviews and be as open as possible
- The components of the review include:
    - A short summary (What did the authors do? How did they do it? What were the results?)
    - An assessment of the major strengths of the paper. Provide details that justify your assessment, whether it is a novel formulation, an original way to use the data, a demonstration of feasibility, a strong evaluation or any other strong aspect
    - An assessment of the major weaknesses. Where possible, provide ideas of what you would have expected or suggest future work to address weak spots. Comments regarding lack of novelty need to be backed up by references to prior work 
    - Specific questions to be address in the rebuttal (if applicable)
    - A final recommendation on whether to accept or reject the paper (with justification)
- We actively support open data/code sharing and request that reviewers check for detailed descriptions of algorithms, parameters, and datasets, though limitations in open data/code availability will not be a reason for rejections. Please refer to <a href=https://www.midl.io/reproducibility> MIDL reproducibility guidelines.</a>
- Reviewers are not required to look at the Supplementary material
- Reviewers should provide a rating for the paper compared to the rest of their stack
- Reviewers are expected to engage in discussion with the authors during the rebuttal period 

<div style="text-align: justify">
Our encouragement is for reviewers to take full responsibility of their work and unblind themselves, making their efforts recognized, increasing the transparency of the process and helping us create a community built on trust and constructive feedback.
</div>

## How to review negative results?

Additional guidelines for the Clinical Translation and Validation Track:

<div style="text-align: justify">
<p><b>Grounded analysis and experimentation.</b> It is important that the negative result not be simply an empirical observation that some experiment did not turn out as expected or hoped. It is important that a negative result be grounded in deeper analysis, whether through a combination of conceptually-informed conjectures and careful experimentation, through rigorous proofs, or some combination.</p> 

<p><b>Changes our understanding of an important question.</b> The significance bar for Negative Results papers is high. The negative result should be informative in changing how the community addresses a question or exposing that the community should be addressing an important question in a different way. A negative result relating to something few people would need or want is not significant.</p> 

<p><b>Unexpected or surprising in some way.</b> The originality bar for Negative Results papers is high. The negative result must be surprising or unexpected in some way - that is, it should run counter to a popularly held understanding. “A linear classifier cannot separate a nonlinear decision boundary” is a negative result that can be rigorously shown (quality) with clean language (clarity) and matters a lot (significance). However, it is not original because readers may be expected to either know this fact or be unsurprised to hear it.</p> 
</div>


## Policy on the use of Large Language Models

<div style="text-align: justify">
MIDL 2027 welcomes the responsible use of modern tools, including Large Language Models (LLMs), when preparing reviews, provided that reviewers maintain scientific integrity, and full responsibility for all content. LLMs and other assistive tools may be used only for minor language edits, such as grammar, spelling, style refinement, readability improvements, or formatting. LLMs must not replace reviewers’ critical thinking.
</div>
